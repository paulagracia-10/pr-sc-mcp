\title{STUDENT PAPER: Efficient Context Engineering for MCP-based LLM Agents: Predictive Tool Routing, Source-Aware Compression, Evidence Packaging and Observable Context Optimization}

% Author(s) info

\author{Paula Gracia}
\affiliation{American School of Madrid}

% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
The Model Context Protocol (MCP) standardizes how large language model (LLM) agents connect to tools, APIs and data sources, but connectivity alone does not solve the problem of context growth: agents that can call many tools tend to retrieve excessive raw output, duplicate context across steps, and pass noisy or weakly relevant evidence into the model. This paper proposes PR-SC MCP, a Predictive Routing and Source-Aware Compression context-governance architecture that treats context as a managed resource across four mechanisms: predictive tool routing, source-aware compression, evidence packaging with anti-lost-in-the-middle ordering, and an observability feedback loop built on an evidence ledger.

The architecture is validated in two stages: an initial Architecture Validation under controlled and semi-real conditions with deterministic scoring, followed by Live Validation against three open-weight models---gpt-oss-120b, gemma-4-31b, and zai-glm-4.7---served on Cerebras infrastructure and evaluated with a single, consistent runner script. Under the regenerated Architecture Validation runner that accompanies this study, PR-SC MCP reduces prompt tokens by 47.1\%, tool calls by 39.1\%, and simulated latency by 27.4\% relative to an exhaustive MCP baseline, while improving deterministic answer accuracy and faithfulness to 2.00 on the 0--2 scoring scale. Under Live Validation, all three models completed the full 10-case $\times$ 4-variant $\times$ 5-run matrix, yielding 200 generations per model and 600 generations in total, with zero recorded errors. Full PR-SC MCP remains near token-neutral relative to the Exhaustive MCP Baseline across all three models, with token overheads of approximately 0.3--1.9\%. It reduces mean latency for gpt-oss-120b and gemma-4-31b, by approximately 26.17\% and 30.24\% respectively, and shows a positive but statistically uncertain mean latency reduction for zai-glm-4.7. Deterministic quality and robustness checks---explicit uncertainty handling, correct stale-versus-fresh evidence prioritization, correct security-distractor handling, and overclaim-risk monitoring---remain at or near-ceiling levels across the tested models. A 60-answer semantic review calibration further found high and tightly clustered semantic scores across variants, with no evidence of quality degradation for Full PR-SC MCP in the reviewed sample. These findings support PR-SC MCP as a context-governance architecture that preserves evidence discipline without introducing a material live-generation efficiency penalty, while also showing that latency gains remain model-dependent.

The central contribution is both architectural and methodological: scalable MCP-based agents benefit from disciplined governance over the full lifecycle of context, and this benefit is not an artifact of any single model or benchmark run---it is supported by a reproducible deterministic Architecture Validation runner and by live validation across three independent open-weight models evaluated under a single consistent methodology.
\end{abstract}
